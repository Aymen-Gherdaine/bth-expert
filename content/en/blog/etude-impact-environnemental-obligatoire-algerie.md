Before launching an industrial, mining, agricultural, or infrastructure project in Algeria, one question systematically arises for project developers: must their file include an Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA), a simple impact notice, or neither? The answer determines the project timeline, the study budget, and ultimately, the obtaining of a building permit or operating authorisation.

## The legal basis

The obligation for environmental assessment stems from Law no. 03-10 of 19 July 2003 on the protection of the environment within the framework of sustainable development, which establishes the general principle. Its implementing provisions are set out in **executive decree no. 07-145 of 19 May 2007**, which defines the scope, content, and approval procedures for [environmental impact assessments and notices](/en/services/etude-impact-environnemental). This text has since been amended and supplemented by executive decrees no. 18-255 (2018) and no. 19-241 (2019), demonstrating that the framework continues to evolve — a development we monitor with each new project.

## Assessment or notice: two sources to check

Article 3 of Decree 07-145 sets the rule. An assessment or notice is required, on the one hand, for classified installations where the nomenclature of Executive Decree No. 07-144 demands it (the nomenclature has, for each heading, an "impact assessment" column and an "impact notice" column), and on the other hand for the projects listed in the two annexes of Decree 07-145, entirely replaced by Decree 19-241 of 2019.

- **Annex I, impact assessment**: for example tourist developments of 10 hectares and more, motorways, airports, ports and dams, warehouses of 20,000 m² and more, hydrocarbon pipelines, exploration or extraction of oil, gas or minerals, hospitals of 500 beds and more, wind farms and solar plants above 20 MW.
- **Annex II, impact notice**: for example the development of business parks and industrial zones, car parks of more than 100 vehicles, power lines above 30 kV, water supply for more than 10,000 inhabitants, shopping centres of 5,000 m² and more.

A plant, a cement works or a hydrocarbon depot do not appear in these annexes: their obligation comes from the nomenclature. Heavy industrial projects ([petrochemicals, cement plants](/en/secteurs/industrie-petrochimie)), [energy and hydrocarbon](/en/secteurs/energie-hydrocarbures) installations, major [infrastructure works](/en/secteurs/infrastructures-btp) and structuring [agricultural and hydraulic](/en/secteurs/agriculture-hydraulique) developments most often require a full impact assessment. Determining the right obligation early on, even before costing the study, avoids over-sizing or under-estimating the file to be submitted.

## What a compliant EIA file contains

Article 6 of the decree, in its 2018 wording, sets the content: presentation of the project owner and of the consultancy with a copy of its accreditation decision, analysis of alternatives, delimitation of the study area according to the posting radius, detailed baseline of the site with a 1:2,500 location plan and a 1:200 site plan, description of the project phases, assessment of direct, indirect and cumulative impacts, measures to eliminate, reduce or offset them with their financial implications, a detailed environmental management plan with a schedule, and a descriptive report of the project made available to the public during the inquiry. At BTH Expert we organise it in four parts:

1. **Site baseline**: inventory of environmental components (physical, biological, human, landscape), from on-site surveys and secondary data.
2. **Impact analysis**: identification, qualification and ranking of direct and indirect, temporary and permanent impacts, during construction and operation.
3. **Mitigation measures**: elimination, reduction or offsetting of significant impacts, each measure being costed and scheduled.
4. **Environmental management plan and monitoring**: monitoring indicators, control frequency, alert thresholds and reporting procedure.

## File processing

The assessment or notice, initialled by the consultancy, is filed with the wali in fourteen paper copies and two digital copies. The environment services examine it within one month and may request additional information, to be provided within two months, failing which the assessment is rejected unless a reasoned extension is requested. After acceptance, the wali opens by order a public inquiry of at most fifteen days, posted and published in two national dailies at the project owner's expense, led by an inquiry commissioner; projects located in industrial zones, business parks, port or bonded zones are exempt. The technical services give their opinion within one month, the project owner replies within ten days, then the file is sent to the minister in charge of the environment, who approves the impact assessment, or to the environment services for the notice, approved by the wali. The decision is notified by the wali; in case of reasoned rejection, an administrative appeal is open to the minister.

An incomplete file, or one that does not follow the regulatory content, systematically triggers a request for additional information, hence a delay on the project schedule, and no construction work may start before approval (Article 21).

## Anticipate rather than react

Best practice consists of integrating the environmental assessment question upstream of project design, rather than treating it as a last-minute formality before submitting a building permit application. An initial site assessment carried out early often makes it possible to adjust the location or process to reduce impacts — and therefore the volume of compensatory measures to be planned.
